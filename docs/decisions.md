# Decisions

Every scope and method decision in this project, with its reason. Copied on 11 Nov 2026 from the working
pick-up doc (claude.ai), where decisions are made session by session; the per-trial and per-name evidence for
each hand review lives in [`data/review/README.md`](../data/review/README.md) and the `*_decided.csv` files next to it.

From 10 Nov on, each decision is written as: options · choice · why · cost (what it might get wrong) · check.
Rows from before 10 Nov have the choice and why only.

## Name review (Phase 1)

Which intervention names count as a GLP-1. Decided per name, not per trial: one trial lists several names.

| Rule | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| GLP-2 drugs (teduglutide etc.) | Exclude | Different hormone; they share the "-glutide" ending |
| GLP-1/GLP-2 duals (dapiglutide) | Keep | They activate the GLP-1 receptor |
| Receptor blockers (exendin 9-39) | Exclude | The opposite of the drug class |
| Native GLP-1 infusions | Exclude | A physiology tool, not a drug anyone develops |
| Measurements, tracers, food products | Exclude | Not a treatment |
| Placebo-only names | Exclude | The trial still gets in through its active arm |
| Insulin + GLP-1 combos (IDegLira, iGlarLixi) | Keep | A real GLP-1 is given |
| Radiolabelled, PK and drug-interaction studies | Keep the name | It's the real drug; the disease map will sort these into "healthy volunteers" |
| Class wording with no drug named | Keep, `canonical_drug` blank | Real GLP-1 treatment; can't feed the drug-generation split (a limitation) |
| Generic names ("glp-1", "glucagon-like peptide-1" and 5 other spellings) | Exclude; rescue real-drug trials in the overrides file | One name covers both the native hormone and real drugs |
| "glp-1 plus basal insulin" | Keep, canonical_drug = lixisenatide | One trial only, and it gives lixisenatide |
| Native GLP-1 given as treatment (ICU, after bypass surgery, PCI) | Exclude | The native hormone can't become a product |
| Trials that raise the body's own GLP-1 (surgery, diet, DPP-4 inhibitors) | Exclude | The trial must give a GLP-1 receptor agonist |
| People already on a GLP-1 from their own doctor | Exclude | Background use, not a drug being tested |
| Trial-level filters (observational, no experimental arm) | Not in the name map; Phase 2's job | Decide names, not trials |
| Non-incretin GLP-1 duals (HEC88473, GLP-1 + FGF21) | Keep | Anything that activates the GLP-1 receptor counts, the same test as the GLP-1/GLP-2 duals |
| One drug under two names (supaglutide, efsubaglutide alfa) | Keep, canonical_drug = efsubaglutide alfa | The sponsor lists Supaglutide as an other name in NCT06732960 |
| Recombinant exendin-4 (rexenatide-4) | Keep, canonical_drug = exenatide | A recombinant copy of exenatide's molecule |
| "rhglp-1(7-36)" in NCT03986008 | Keep, canonical_drug = beinaglutide | The trial title names benaglutide; the 7-36 label looks like native GLP-1 but isn't |
| Evidence order for unfamiliar names | AACT text first, the web only if it's silent | The sponsor's own record is the primary source; AI summaries can be wrong (vurolenatide) |
| Placebo + active names | Keep when a real GLP-1 is given (A: another drug's placebo; C: blinded label or crossover). Exclude when the GLP-1 is the placebo (B) | The name decides whether that arm gets a real GLP-1. Cagrilintide alone isn't a GLP-1, so "cagrilintide B and placebo semaglutide I" is excluded |
| Stopping and tapering | Keep when the protocol tapers the dose or pauses and restarts it. Exclude a full-stop arm or a stop before a procedure | Same test as always: does this arm get a GLP-1 the study assigns? A stop arm gets none; a pre-procedure stop tests the procedure |
| Patients' own GLP-1, changed by the protocol | Keep. Who supplies the pen doesn't matter | NCT07387796 pauses and restarts tirzepatide by protocol and measures its effects (keep); NCT07623148 assigns nothing (exclude) |
| Comparator arm with a protocol-fixed GLP-1 dose | Keep the name | Same as NCT07783854's maintenance arm, which rule 11 already keeps; Phase 2's arm filter decides the trial |
| GLP-1 backbone in every arm, another drug tested on top | Keep the name. In Phase 2, count trials with a GLP-1 in both an experimental and a comparator arm; state it as a limitation | "GLP-1 in every arm" also matches single-arm and dose-comparison trials, so no clean SQL rule; most backbone trials sit in obesity |
| Dual names that only describe the drug ("dual glp-1/glp-2 receptor agonists" and 2 others) | Keep, canonical_drug = dapiglutide | All three trials test ZP7570; AACT lists ZP7570 as an other name for dapiglutide (NCT05788601) |
| Name map columns | drug_name, decision, canonical_drug, reason; no n_trials | A lookup table keeps only stable columns; n_trials changes with each snapshot and must never be added up |

## Project-wide decisions

| Rule | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Academic vs industry (lead sponsor agency_class) | Industry = INDUSTRY. Academic = OTHER, OTHER_GOV, NIH, FED, NETWORK, INDIV. Unclassified = UNKNOWN, AMBIG, NULL ([Redacted]): count and report | Every trial has exactly one lead row (609,804 leads for 609,804 trials), so lead sponsor gives one row per trial. OTHER_GOV holds public hospitals and research councils; NIH and FED are public funders. Only 90 leads are UNKNOWN, but UNKNOWN collaborators can hide industry in the collaborator split |
| WITHDRAWN trials (never enrolled) | Exclude from main counts (Q1–Q3). Report the withdrawn count per area. Sensitivity check: rerun Q2 with them included | A withdrawn trial is a plan, not activity, often dropped for funding or recruitment. Counting it would inflate academic numbers in the white-space areas and could set a false first-trial year in Q1. Decided 9 Nov |
| White space (decision 2) | An area is white space when its industry-led share of 2023–2025 trial starts is below half the baseline share, and no industry-led Phase 3 is active. Baseline = industry-led share of all GLP-1 trials outside diabetes and obesity, same window. Apply a minimum-trials threshold. Sensitivity check: rerun at one third and two thirds of baseline. Runs at the indication level (see the next row) | Zero industry trials is too strict: 3 industry vs 50 academic is still an opening. A fixed % is arbitrary, so anchor it to the field. An active industry Phase 3 means a competitor is already close, whatever the share. Decided 9 Nov |
| Growth and the COVID dip | Keep the windows 2023–2025 vs 2020–2022. An area is heating up only if it grew faster than GLP-1 trials outside diabetes and obesity as a whole. Limitation wording: "Trial starts in 2020–2021 were lower than normal because COVID-19 paused or delayed many trials. Growth is therefore measured against the overall trend of GLP-1 trials outside diabetes and obesity, not as raw change, so an area only counts as heating up if it grew faster than the field." | COVID depressed the earlier window and the post-2021 Wegovy boom lifts every area, so raw growth flatters everyone. Decided 9 Nov |
| Disease level for the analysis | The unit is the indication, as a drug label would name it (e.g. Parkinson's, MASH, HFpEF, sleep apnoea). No roll-up: an indication below the minimum-trials threshold stays its own row, flagged "too early to call". Merge conditions that are one disease spectrum into one indication (e.g. NAFLD, NASH, MASLD, MASH) and write down every merge. Broad groups only for the hero chart | Pharma develops and partners one indication at a time, so it's the BD reader's own unit. One consistent level, so every shortlist row means the same thing. Rolling small indications into broad groups would hide brand-new openings. Replaces the 4 Nov roll-up idea for the white-space test. Decided 9 Nov |
| Drug generation split | Split generic vs branded, not older vs newer. Generic = other companies sell approved copies, so a university can just buy it. Branded = only the original company sells it, so a trial usually needs that company's help. Judged by US or EU status on the snapshot date (3 Nov 2026), since status differs by country. Generic: exenatide, liraglutide, plus albiglutide (withdrawn 2018, few trials; note it). Branded: dulaglutide, semaglutide, tirzepatide and next-wave drugs. Classify by drug, not by status at trial start; state as a limitation | The split exists to separate cheap, easy-access drugs from company-supplied ones (a vote with real money), so generic status is the direct measure. Liraglutide was branded until recently, so older liraglutide trials weren't cheap: accepted for simplicity. Decided 9 Nov |
| NOT_YET_RECRUITING trials (registered, no one enrolled yet) | Active. Applies in Q3's status buckets and in the white-space test's "no active industry-led Phase 3" | For a BD team, a company registering a Phase 3 is the strongest sign it's coming in, because it has already committed the money. Decided 9 Nov |
| Q3 status buckets | Active = RECRUITING, NOT_YET_RECRUITING, ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, ENROLLING_BY_INVITATION. Completed = COMPLETED. Stopped = TERMINATED, SUSPENDED. Unknown = UNKNOWN. WITHDRAWN excluded. The white-space test uses the same "active" list | Every status code needs one bucket. SUSPENDED is paused, not enrolling, so stopped; no late-stage GLP-1 trial is suspended now (2 GLP-1 trials in all, Phase 4 and Early Phase 1). Stopped trials then go through the why_stopped split (4 Nov). Decided 9 Nov |
| UNKNOWN status (end date passed, no update for 2+ years) | Keep in Q1 and Q2 counts; own "unknown" bucket in Q3. Sensitivity check if time allows: rerun Q2 without UNKNOWN, batched with the other Q2 reruns in Week 4 | A start date is a fact even when the ending isn't known. Rough preview: 139 of ~1,860 GLP-1 trials are UNKNOWN, 13.2% of non-industry-led vs 1.9% of industry-led, so dropping them would shrink academic counts and hide white space. Limitation: some may never have started. Decided 9 Nov |
| 'NA' and PHASE4 trials in Q3 | Count in Q1 and Q2. Leave out of both Q3 phase measures (first Phase 2 to first Phase 3, and the Phase 2-or-later share): out of the top and the bottom of the share. Report the 'NA' and Phase 4 counts per area | The label doesn't show the real stage. 156 of 160 'NA' GLP-1 trials are non-industry, many of them ordinary drug RCTs. 355 of 405 Phase 4 trials are non-industry, often a marketed drug tried in a new disease, which is early work. Counting Phase 4 as late would flatter academic areas. Decided 9 Nov |
| Arm rule (Option B) | Keep a trial if the GLP-1 is in an EXPERIMENTAL arm, or if the trial has no EXPERIMENTAL arm and the GLP-1 is in any arm. Label each trial with arm_rule; the strict rule (experimental arm only) is the sensitivity check | 269 interventional trials have no experimental arm, 92% non-industry, including Exenatide-PD3 (NCT04232969): sponsors label arms loosely. The rule still drops the 92 true comparator-only trials. Decided 9 Nov |
| Trials with both a generic and a branded GLP-1 (drug generation split) | Count as branded if any branded drug is in an EXPERIMENTAL arm. Report the mixed count (generic + branded in experimental arms) per area so the reader can see it | Why: the split exists to catch company-backed trials, and a branded drug in a tested arm usually means the company supplied it. Cost: a trial that mostly tests a cheap generic with one small branded arm may be just an academic study, so the rule can overstate company-backed interest and make the white space look stronger. First "I direct, Claude reviews" decision. Decided 10 Nov |
| Scouting label for white-space indications (academic trials with an industry partner) | Keep the white-space test as is (industry-led share). Add a label per shortlisted indication: "open" or "being scouted", based on its academic trials with an industry collaborator. Show which companies collaborate and how many trials each backs in that indication. The three trial sponsor groups don't change. Rule for "being scouted": only collaborators that are big pharma GLP-1 makers count (e.g. Novo, Lilly; not device firms). Scouting share = academic trials with such a collaborator / all academic trials in the indication, 2023–2025. Being scouted = share above the baseline (same share across all GLP-1 trials outside diabetes and obesity, same window); else open. No label below the minimum-trials threshold: "too early to call", as in the indication rule. Why a share: a fixed count proves little. Cost: just above the threshold, one trial can still flip the label (decided 10 Nov) | Why: big pharma backing academic trials is a real signal (money, speed, connections) that a pure academic/industry split hides, and dropping it would be a loss. A label beats weighting, because a weight (half? a third?) is arbitrary. Cost: Novo and Lilly give drug to many academic studies as routine support, and a collaborator may not be a GLP-1 maker (e.g. a device firm), so the label can overstate scouting. Check: the company breakdown; one GLP-1 maker across many trials is a signal, one random firm isn't. Raised in a BD-director review. Decided 10 Nov |
| The "so what": is a white-space indication worth anything? | After Q2, add one line per shortlisted indication: patient numbers and whether a good treatment already exists, each with a source. Source: Global Burden of Disease (IHME) first; the latest systematic review or meta-analysis where GBD doesn't split the indication (e.g. sleep apnoea, HFpEF), noted. Treatments: FDA or EMA approvals. One region for all (US or global: to set). About 30 min per indication, 2–4 h total. Also keep a limitation line: this isn't market sizing. If behind schedule: skip the lines and state it as a limitation only (15 min) | Why: trial counts show activity, not commercial value; a BD director first asks how many patients and whether a treatment exists. Option chosen over limitation only (no insight) and full market sizing (15+ h, paid data, a second project). Cost: prevalence varies by source, country and definition, so mixed sources mislead, and the numbers aren't reproducible from the repo. Check: one source type and one region throughout, every figure cited, timeboxed. Raised in a BD-director review. Decided 10 Nov |
| Which sensitivity checks to run (cut from 5) | Keep 3: white-space threshold at 1/3 and 2/3 of baseline; strict experimental-arm-only rule; PHASE2/PHASE3 counted as Phase 3 (watchlist). Instead of rerunning Q2 without UNKNOWN, report the UNKNOWN count per shortlisted indication (full rerun only if time allows). Drop the WITHDRAWN rerun. In the write-up, mark which indications stay on the shortlist through all 3 checks. Replaces the sensitivity checks in the WITHDRAWN and UNKNOWN rows | Why: 10 h a week; keep only checks that could change the shortlist or watchlist. The threshold tests the headline rule; the arm rule changes the trial list under all 3 questions, mostly academic trials, where white space lives; phase 2/3 is the watchlist's only check and cheap (one CASE change). WITHDRAWN trials never enrolled and are few. A BD director most fears a false lead, and an indication propped up by UNKNOWN trials is one, so its UNKNOWN count is shown. Cost: fewer checks, so some fragility goes untested. Raised in a BD-director review. Decided 10 Nov |
| Recall gap: drugs named only by a development code (e.g. HEC88473, BMS-686117) | Search interventions.description for GLP-1 wording, only for trials not already in the list. Review the new hits by hand, as in the name review, with a time limit (about 2 h). Too many hits → state it as a limitation instead. Do it in Phase 2, before the trial list is final | Why: next-wave industry drugs often appear only as codes, so the gap mostly misses industry trials and can make industry look absent: a false white-space lead. Narrowed to descriptions of new trials because free text across titles and summaries is bulky and noisy (GLP-1 levels, DPP-4 inhibitors, background mentions). Cost: codes with no GLP-1 wording anywhere still slip through; hand review takes time. Raised in a BD-director review. Decided 10 Nov |
| BMS-686117 (NCT00508287) | Keep: add bms-686117 to glp1_name_additions.csv, canonical_drug = bms-686117 | Why: an 11-mer GLP-1 receptor agonist from Bristol-Myers Squibb (NCATS); AACT is silent on what it is, so the name search missed it. Byetta in that trial is only the comparator, so Option B rightly left the trial out until the tested drug was named. Cost: none; Phase 1 in type 2 diabetes, home base. Headline 1,845 → 1,846. Decided 10 Nov |
| "Group a" triple-combination arms (NCT07635953, NCT06946628) | Options: keep through overrides, or exclude as a treatment strategy (pattern B). Chose keep, through the overrides file: canonical_drug = tirzepatide (NCT07635953), semaglutide (NCT06946628) | Why: the tested arm gets a named GLP-1 from day one, at doses set by the protocol, with empagliflozin and pioglitazone. That's the combo rule (IDegLira, CagriSema), not a strategy where the GLP-1 is one option or a later step. Two sister trials, one fixed drug each, so not doctor's choice. Cost: the trial tests the combination, so the GLP-1's own effect can't be separated. Check: none; both are newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes, home base. Headline 1,846 → 1,848. Decided 10 Nov |
| "glp-1 cellbeads" (NCT01298830, industry) | Options: keep as an industry GLP-1 product, or exclude as native GLP-1. Chose exclude | Why: a cell therapy (implanted capsules of cells that secrete native GLP-1 after stroke surgery), not a GLP-1 receptor agonist drug; the BD reader develops GLP-1 drugs and wouldn't partner on or compete with a cell implant. The old native-GLP-1 reason ("nobody develops it") doesn't hold here, since a company does. Cost: an industry trial in stroke, a possible new area, so stroke looks slightly more open to industry than it is. Check: in Phase 3, if stroke makes the shortlist, name this trial in its row. Decided 10 Nov |
| Where the WITHDRAWN filter goes | Dropped once, in glp1_trials (Step 4 of 02): headline 1,809. The 39 withdrawn trials (26 non-industry-led, 13 industry-led) go in one limitation line; no per-area withdrawn count (replaces that part of the 9 Nov WITHDRAWN row) | Why: one filter in one place, so every Phase 4 query starts from the same list and none can quietly disagree; at 0–2 per indication, the per-area count was noise. Non-industry trials are withdrawn at twice the industry rate (2.9% vs 1.4%). Cost: no per-area withdrawn count. Decided 11 Nov |
| Indication mapping (Phase 3, Review 6) | MeSH terms tagged home / real / broad / measure (renamed from notd, 11 Nov) / error; 20 indications. Type 1 diabetes is new, not home; prediabetes and gestational diabetes are home; drug studies (PK, healthy volunteers) get their own reported bucket (option A); a title keyword pass per indication catches population-tagged trials (option B). Full rules, why and cost per row: data/review/README.md, Review 6 | Why: the term-level evidence lives next to the CSV in the repo; this row points to it. Decided 11 Nov |
| Where the indication mapping lives | Options: a VALUES list in the SQL (the plan) or a CSV. Chose the CSV: move 03_mesh_terms_tagged.csv to data/reference/ and load it in 02a, like the name map | Why: one source of truth, and easy to add terms later (the 205 untagged ones). Cost: the mapping isn't visible in the query, and 02a must be rerun after each pull (already the routine). Decided 11 Nov |
| Heart-outcome trials (ASCVD vs home base) | Heart-outcome trials without diabetes (SELECT) → ASCVD. Exception to tag-by-target: heart-outcome trials in type 2 diabetes (e.g. LEADER) stay home base. Keyword pass: the ASCVD phrases count as hits; titles with only the plain word "cardiovascular" go on a short hand-review list | Why: regulators have required heart-outcome trials for every new diabetes drug since 2008, so in diabetes they are routine, not a move into heart disease. Plain "cardiovascular" is too broad (catches heart failure, blood pressure, risk-factor studies). Decided 11 Nov |
| Stroke split | Preventing stroke (part of a heart-attack/stroke/CV-death outcome, as in SELECT) → ASCVD. Treating a stroke during or after it (brain protection, recovery; e.g. exenatide in acute stroke, cellbeads) → its own indication, Stroke | Why: different patients and different endpoints (cardiology vs neurology). Cost: "stroke" alone can't decide it, so those titles go to hand review. Decided 11 Nov |
| measure desk check (Review 6) | Retag weight loss, body weight, body weight changes → home. Keep hypoglycemia, weight gain, insulin resistance, inflammation, motor activity as measure. Two single trials fixed by hand in data/reference/indication_additions.csv: NCT02527993 → post-bariatric hypoglycemia (new indication), NCT00845507 → antipsychotic-induced weight gain | Why: retag a term only when it names what's treated across most of its trials; an odd trial is fixed by hand, not the term. 11 trials had weight loss as their only tag (incl. 4 VK2735 Phase 3s). Tagging hypoglycemia real would put diabetes safety trials in a hypoglycemia area. Cost: NCT06518837 (tirzepatide, breast cancer) sits in home only until the title pass. Decided 11 Nov |
| MeSH tags vs title keywords | A trial's indications = its MeSH tags + its title keyword hits, combined. The title pass only adds indications; it never removes a tag. A trial counts in each of its indications, never summed. Accepted title hits go in data/reference/indication_additions.csv with a reason | Why: sponsors often list only the population as the condition. STEP-HFpEF (NCT04788511) is tagged obesity only, but enrols heart failure patients and its primary outcome is KCCQ, a heart-failure score; tags alone would hide Novo in heart failure, a false white-space lead. Cost: keyword hits need hand review. Check: keywords written before looking at hits; known-trials check. Decided 11 Nov |
| Phase 3 merges, splits and coverage fixes | All merges and splits in one log at the end of data/review/README.md (Merge and split log), with Reviews 7–8. Tag notd renamed measure; new tag error for MeSH mistakes (lymphoma, follicular). Drug study is a label, not a removal: Phase 4 ignores a trial's other labels when it carries it | Why: one place to check every merge, next to the evidence CSVs, as the 9 Nov rule asks (write down every merge). Decided 11 Nov |
| Second target in the official title (Review 9) | A trial gets an indication its official title names as a target and its outcomes measure, even when the primary outcome is another indication. NCT06409130 (Novo, alcohol-related liver disease) also gets Alcohol use disorder | Why: Novo names alcohol use in the official title and measures it twice (PEth, TLFB). Cost: AUD looks less open if Novo only tracks drinking as a side effect. Decided 11 Nov |
| Alzheimer's vs cognitive impairment in diabetes (Review 9) | Alzheimer's only when the trial names Alzheimer's (title, conditions, MeSH) or enrols by an Alzheimer's diagnosis. Cognitive impairment or MCI in diabetes without that → new indication Cognitive impairment in diabetes (LIGHT-MCI, LIGHT-COG, NCT02847403 moved from Alzheimer's) | Why: the 9 Nov no-roll-up rule; academic MCI trials would lower Alzheimer's industry share and make it look more open; on approved labels MCI is a stage of Alzheimer's, so MCI without that diagnosis is its own area. Cost: LIGHT-COG uses Alzheimer's criteria and endpoints, so Alzheimer's may miss one academic Phase 3. Decided 11 Nov |
| Weight loss in a disease population (Review 9) | Counts as that disease (PCOS, breast cancer: FITWISE), the same as the breast cancer trials already labelled | Why: consistent with existing labels; the trial is run because of the disease. Cost: some may be plain obesity trials in that population, which flatters academic activity there. Decided 11 Nov |
